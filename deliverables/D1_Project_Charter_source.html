1. Business Context and Stakeholders

Every trading day on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) opens with a pre-open call auction (09:00–09:15 IST) in which the opening price is discovered. The opening price frequently differs from the previous day's close: news, quarterly results, global market moves and overnight sentiment all arrive while the market is shut. Traders, portfolio managers and treasury desks must decide before the open whether to place, hold or re-price orders, and today they mostly rely on the simple assumption that "tomorrow opens where today closed" or on informal judgement.

2. Problem Statement

Decision: Before the NSE opens on day t+1, how should a trader price pre-open orders for a given stock?
Analytics question: Using only information available at the close of day t (that day's OHLCV bar, recent price history, market-wide movement and the latest published company fundamentals), can we predict the opening price of each stock on day t+1 more accurately than the naive "open = previous close" rule, and give a reliable range around that prediction?

Problem type: supervised regression on panel time-series data. The model predicts the overnight gap, (Opent+1 ÷ Closet) − 1, which is then converted to a rupee opening price. Predicting the gap rather than the raw price keeps the target comparable across stocks of very different price levels and avoids a model that merely copies yesterday's close.

3. Success Metrics and Acceptance Criteria

All metrics are measured out of sample with walk-forward (expanding-window) validation over at least 5 time-ordered folds. The benchmark is the naive baseline Opent+1 = Closet; a second baseline uses the historical mean gap from the training window.

MetricDefinitionAcceptance criterion
Mean Absolute Error (₹)Average absolute difference between predicted and actual opening priceLower than the naive baseline on average across folds, and in at least 4 of 5 folds
MAPE (%)Mean absolute error as a percentage of the actual openAt least 5% relative improvement over the naive baseline (e.g. 0.60% → 0.57% or better)
Direction accuracy (%)Share of days on which the sign of the predicted gap (up/down) is correct, excluding flat opensAbove 55%, and significantly above 50% (binomial test, p < 0.05)
Range coverageShare of actual opens that fall inside the predicted 10–90% bandBetween 75% and 85% (target 80%)
Statistical significanceDiebold–Mariano test of model errors vs baseline errorsModel better than baseline at the 5% level
OperationalEnd-to-end pipeline run after 16:00 ISTPredictions for all stocks ready before 09:00 IST next day; fully reproducible from the repository

Note: the naive baseline is hard to beat because most overnight gaps are small and close to random. If the model does not clear these thresholds, the team will report this honestly, together with an analysis of where (which stocks, which market conditions) any predictive signal does or does not exist.

Features (all known at day t's close)

1-, 5- and 20-day returns; intraday return; today's gap; high–low range; position of close within the day's range; volume relative to its 20-day average; 20-day volatility; distance from 20-day moving average; day of week; market-wide return and gap; and the latest fundamentals (log market cap, P/E, P/B, EPS, ROE, debt-to-equity).

Preparation, quality and leakage controls

  • Time-aware validation only: walk-forward splits; no random shuffling; at least 250 trading days of training history before the first test fold.
  • Point-in-time fundamentals: backward-only as-of join on the announcement date, so no quarter's numbers reach days before publication.
  • Corporate actions: days with overnight moves above ±20% (splits, bonuses, data errors) are flagged and excluded or adjusted.
  • Missing data and holidays: gaps checked against the NSE calendar; missing fundamentals handled natively by the model (gradient boosting) rather than imputed with future values.
  • Bias: survivorship bias acknowledged (current large caps only); large-cap results may not generalise to small caps.
  • Versioning: raw and processed data stored by date in the team repository with a datasheet (D2).

Modelling approach (preview of D4)

Baselines first (open = previous close; historical mean gap), then linear regression with regularisation, then gradient-boosted trees (scikit-learn HistGradientBoosting, absolute-error loss). The uncertainty band comes from out-of-sample residual quantiles. Experiments are logged with parameters and fold-wise results.

Responsible data handling

The project uses only public market and company-level data; no personal data is collected, so the DPDP Act, 2023 consent requirements are not triggered. Data is used within each provider's licence terms (Bloomberg data stays within the institutional licence and is not redistributed). API keys are kept in an environment variable and never stored in code, documents or the repository. Outputs are presented as an analytical exercise, not investment advice.

Feasibility

Feasibility is high. The data is public and affordable, and a working prototype pipeline (data fetch, feature build, walk-forward training, prediction and Streamlit dashboard) already exists. It has so far been tested end to end on synthetic data only, so performance on real market data is still to be established.
